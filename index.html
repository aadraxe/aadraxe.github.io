About

Tampa, Florida

I'm a senior in Computer Science at USF, graduating in May 2027. Since February 2025 I've worked in the RARE Lab with Prof. Zhao Han, building the voice, vision, and planning pipeline for a companion robot and helping run controlled user studies with Fetch and Pepper robots.

I also work as a cybersecurity analyst at Cyber Florida. A prompt-injection classifier I built there scored well on familiar attacks and missed most unfamiliar ones, so I built AegisFlow, which enforces an agent's limits outside the model instead of trusting it to spot attacks. My PhD goal is to bring both halves together: robots whose limits are set by the people around them, hold under attack, and come with a reason when they refuse.

Research

HRI · figures use our results

CRIE: robots that explain their intent

Learned manipulation policies that succeed 99% of the time alone drop to 75 to 81% beside a human partner, and 44% of coordination failures come from people and robots waiting on or duplicating each other. CRIE adds a vision-language planner that splits a task between human and robot and turns the robot's plan, progress, and uncertainty into spoken and projected explanations.

My part: Bloom's speech and LLM communication layer, camera capture, and a task-detection component that sends task requests and camera data to the planner over gRPC.

Can an AR robot replace a real one?

Mobile manipulators like Fetch cost tens of thousands of dollars, which limits who can run and replicate HRI studies. We compared a physical Fetch with a fully virtual AR Fetch (HoloLens 2) in a collaborative assembly task with 33 participants. Accuracy and trust showed evidence of no difference, but people took about twice as long to read the virtual robot's intent.

My part: co-first author. Programmed the Fetch in ROS, fixed the AR virtual-robot setup, helped run sessions, and now lead the revision.

Deterring robot abuse with protective gear

We tested whether armor, headgear, or a shield on a Pepper robot changes how people judge the likelihood and severity of abusing it. Each of 32 participants experienced every design from both an abuser's and a bystander's perspective. Abusers rated headgear as more effective than the shield at reducing severity.

My part: conceptualization, methodology, software, data curation, visualization, and writing the original draft. I helped redesign the experiment, reprogrammed Pepper for the new protocol, and ran the study sessions. Paper

Bloom, a companion robot for older adults

The robot platform the CRIE work runs on. I built its on-device interaction stack: wake-word speech recognition (Vosk), LLM dialogue with text-to-speech and configurable pauses, real-time YOLO object detection on a Hailo accelerator, and a live control dashboard. Presented twice to university faculty and funders.

AegisFlow

Security · try it

An AI agent with tools will eventually read something written by an attacker: an email, a web page, a document. AegisFlow sits between the agent and its tools. Every tool call must pass default-deny policy checks, whatever the model believes, and it tracks where each argument came from, so data from an untrusted source can't quietly become an action.

It started as an LLM-security middleware project my manager at Cyber Florida suggested for the summer. The first defense was a classifier that tried to spot attacks. When it missed most unfamiliar ones, I moved the enforcement outside the model. It builds on ideas from CaMeL and FIDES. Code on GitHub

    Why not just detect attacks?

    My fine-tuned DistilBERT classifier beat an LLM judge on attacks like the ones it was trained on. On an out-of-distribution benchmark, partly in German, it caught only 22% of attacks, fewer than the judge. Detection can help, but it shouldn't be the last line of defense.
